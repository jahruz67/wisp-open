The Experience

From thought to text,
without leaving your keyboard.

No context switching. No browser dashboards. No copy-pasting. Just speak and watch your words appear.

Step 01

Hold & Speak

Hold your global shortcut (Ctrl+Super / Ctrl+Win / Ctrl+Cmd). A sleek, floating audio wave overlay appears on screen showing live microphone input while you speak.

Global keybind configurable in Settings
Step 02

Transcribe & Refine

Release the keys. Audio is routed instantly to your chosen engine: local Whisper/Parakeet for zero-data leakage, or Groq/Mistral for sub-second cloud transcription. Optional LLM refinement cleans grammar and removes filler words.

Pulsing gold HUD during transcription
Step 03

Direct-Typed Anywhere

Wisp directly types the completed text into whichever application was active. Uses native direct input hooks (ydotool on Linux, Win32 on Windows, CGEvent on macOS) without touching your clipboard.

Works in IDEs, chats, browsers & terminals

Linux First-Class Citizen

Engineered natively for Wayland & X11.

Unlike web wrappers that struggle with Linux desktop integration, Wisp ships native `.deb` and `.rpm` packages with an automated `ydotool` systemd user service, `/dev/uinput` permissions, automatic audio muting via `pactl`, and universal shortcut capture that works identically across GNOME, KDE Plasma, and tiling window managers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to common questions.

Is Wisp really 100% free and open source? +
Yes. Wisp (WIS Free V3) is distributed under the permissive MIT License. There are no subscriptions, no locked tiers, no accounts required, and no hidden telemetry.
Do I need an API key to use Wisp? +
No. You can download any of the offline models directly within Settings (Whisper, NVIDIA Parakeet TDT, or Moonshine) and run 100% offline with zero external network requests. If you want ultra-fast cloud inference, you can optionally paste your free Groq or Mistral API key.
How does direct typing work in any application? +
Wisp injects keystrokes natively into whichever window had focus right before you released the hotkey. On Linux, it uses a persistent ydotool daemon and /dev/uinput; on Windows, it uses Win32 input events; on macOS, it uses CGEvent Accessibility hooks. You never need to switch apps or press Paste.
Can I customize the recording shortcut? +
Yes. While the default is Ctrl+Super (Ctrl+Win on Windows, Ctrl+Cmd on macOS), you can click Record in the Wisp Settings window and press any shortcut combination you prefer.
Does offline transcription support GPU acceleration? +
Yes. The bundled whisper.cpp engine includes a Vulkan backend on Windows and Linux, supporting NVIDIA, AMD, and Intel GPUs out of the box without requiring manual driver compilation. On Apple Silicon macOS, Metal acceleration is used.
